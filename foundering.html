Foundering

A survival-horror roguelite aboard a sinking ocean liner. Start deep in the flooding hull and climb toward the boats — managing cold, breath, light, and the people you meet — before the water, the pressure behind the watertight doors, and the ship itself finish you.

It is about generating the right feelings: despair, then hope; cold and alone in the wet dark, and then you find someone. The ship sinks over hours, not minutes — a deliberate climb, not a mad rush — while a clock ticks toward 2:20 AM and the water reads how far she has gone under.

Four ways to play

  • Voyage — the roguelite. A branching run through ten distinct sections of the ship; at every exit you choose to climb toward the surface, hold the deck and head aft, or descend deeper, while the flood persists and worsens. A cross-section between levels shows where you are.
  • Endless — the same climb, without end; keep going as long as you can.
  • Titanic — a hand-authored, historically-educational passage through the real 1912 ship, room by room, with the history switched on.
  • Tutorial — learn the ropes at your own pace.

Eight ways to sail

  • Steerage Passenger — start low, climb, reach a boat. The pure escape.
  • Ship's Officer — your authority forces locked doors, but you cannot leave until you have seen enough souls to safety.
  • Stoker & Engineer — fire the furnace or work the board to hold the power and drive the pumps, outrunning a breach the others cannot.
  • Purser, Mail Clerk, Wireless Operator & a First-Class passenger — keys and coin, the registered post, the distress key — and a passenger who barely believes she is sinking at all.

What's in it

  • Real water — a communicating-vessels flood that settles into the lowest rooms first; open a door and the sea rushes through, or pile furniture in a hatch to dam it and buy time.
  • The ship as a machine — power is a network of circuits; a flooding wing goes dark on its own, and you can reroute a dry wing's power to a drowned one — or triage a failing generator as she goes under.
  • An AI storyteller — a Narrator paces your run and reacts to how you play, and there is more than one — each a personality you can lean into or fight. Some you must earn.
  • Belief & denial — keep your nerve and lead calm souls and the ship feels — and behaves — warmer and slower; let panic in and the cold comes with it.
  • A physical, buoyant world — furniture lifts and drifts as a room floods; grab, carry, and shove it, and brace when the ship lurches and flings everything loose.
  • A survival web — the freezing water drains your warmth and the cold can kill you, not just drowning; duck into boiler-room heat, or drink to hold off the chill.
  • A whole ship to play in — rewire junctions, tap the Marconi key, develop a plate in the darkroom, take the squash court, win a fencing duel, cross a flooded pool on floating planks — and more.
  • Runs that recombine — seeded, always-solvable levels, shareable seeds, leaderboards, dozens of achievements — and early drop-in co-op with friends.

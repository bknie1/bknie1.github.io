Foundering

A survival-horror roguelite aboard a sinking ocean liner. Start deep in the flooding hull and climb toward the boats — managing cold, breath, light, and the people you meet — before the water, the pressure behind the watertight doors, and the ship itself finish you.

It is about generating the right feelings: despair, then hope; cold and alone in the wet dark, and then you find someone. The ship sinks over hours, not minutes — a deliberate climb, not a mad rush — while a clock ticks toward 2:20 AM and the water reads how far she has gone under.

Two ways to play

  • Voyage — the roguelite. A branching run through ten distinct sections of the ship; at every exit you choose to climb toward the surface, hold the deck and head aft, or descend deeper, while the flood persists and worsens. A ship cross-section between levels shows where you are.
  • True Titanic — a hand-authored, historically-educational passage through the real ship, room by room.

Choose your passage

  • Steerage Passenger — start low, climb, reach a boat. The pure escape.
  • Ship's Officer — your authority forces locked doors, but you cannot leave until you have seen enough souls to safety.
  • Stoker — fire the coal furnace to hold the power and drive the pumps, outrunning a breach the others cannot.

What's in it

  • Real water — a communicating-vessels flood that spreads evenly and settles into the lowest rooms first; open a door and the sea rushes through, or pile furniture in a hatch to dam the flow and buy time.
  • Physical, buoyant world — furniture rests dry, then lifts and drifts as a room floods; grab, carry, and shove it. The ship lurches when a new compartment gives way, throwing everything loose.
  • The wet dark — flooded sections lose their lights; the power can cut for seconds and flicker back, lit only by a cursor-aimed flashlight, over synthesized hull groans and water slaps.
  • A survival web — the freezing water drains your warmth and the cold can kill you, not just drowning; duck into the boiler-room heat, or drink to hold off the chill.
  • People, with roles — passengers mill in the public rooms; rescue a few and they follow and help (a worker patches leaks, a steward steadies the panicked), while others are past reason.
  • Watertight doors & class-coded rooms — crank a bulkhead wheel, seal the way behind you, and read a fogged, self-annotating map that marks what still gates you.
  • Seeded, always-solvable levels — a graph check guarantees a route out.
  • Educational mode — the real history of each area, handled with care.

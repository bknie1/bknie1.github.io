OASIS: a student records platform, owned end to end

UC Davis, 2021 to present. Sole full-time engineer and technical lead.

Degree audit, degree certification, academic petitions, student records, and advising workflows for every undergraduate college. Degree certification decides whether a student graduates, so the correctness bar is absolute.

I designed the integration architecture across five institutional systems, two of them third-party with no cooperation available, covering authentication and access scoping, data governance for student PII, failure handling, and loop prevention on bidirectional sync. Sensitive student data sits behind a deliberate break-the-glass step, built to meet the university's security mandate and accepted by advisors once they heard why.

The first thing an advisor sees about a student is a panel I shaped with designated stakeholders from each college. Badges generated from criteria such as college, program, student status, and alerts lead to reports of every student who meets the same criteria, so a glance at one student becomes a path to the others like them.

Three years of that work delivered the Graduation Application, the Change of Program Petition, two exception petitions, and Degree Certification, directly with the University Registrar and the directors of each college. Technical direction for 14 contributors; mentees now at Google, Deloitte, and Chewy.

Degree certification, rebuilt on a domain layer

UC Davis. The decision that settles whether a student graduates.

Degree certification is where a college decides, requirement by requirement, whether a student has earned the degree. A wrong or late answer means a student does not graduate on time, so every decision has to be attributable and explainable.

I replaced a decade of legacy audit logic with a two-layer C# domain that separates persistence from domain reasoning. It carries a decision state model, authorization scoped to the requirement, an event-driven notification service, and an append-only SQL history of every decision. Each college writes and manages its own notification templates without me.

The interface is assembled from the design system's controls: a certification summary, a decision card for each requirement, and the message history alongside.

A faceless API, and the first client that needs no web page

2026. Prototype.

A REST service on ASP.NET Core that lets other systems use the platform's data without touching the web application: per-client hashed keys with scoped roles, Entra ID client credentials planned for production, and every request written to an audit log. The first consumers are advising metrics and an appointment check-in kiosk built in .NET MAUI on the Blazor component library, so a kiosk looks like the rest of the platform without a second copy of the design system.

The same month I wrote the unit's build-or-buy analysis for a two-way sync between the campus incident system and the team's work tracking: every commercial option cost money, routed student data through a vendor cloud, or needed an install in a system we do not control, so the recommendation was an in-house job over the two REST APIs at no new spending. The report also proposed the unit's AI architecture: a locally hosted model behind a compartmentalized service so student data stays in the environment, with AI used at build time to produce deterministic code and rules.

Diagnosing why delivery cost what it cost, then fixing it

2024 to 2026.

I analyzed the full two-year delivery record and quantified how unevenly the work was distributed, using Lorenz curves and Gini coefficients alongside estimation coverage, defect share, and cadence variance. I presented the findings and the corrections to executive leadership with the computation shown, so every figure could be checked rather than trusted.

The delivery structure I then designed moved estimation coverage from 24 percent to 100 percent and workload concentration from 0.64 to 0.25. I facilitate the stand-ups, sprint planning, and retrospectives myself, and the same instruments now run on every sprint. The test infrastructure I stood up from zero (unit, browser-driven end-to-end, and visual regression in one harness, with a coverage audit against a 58 area behavior catalogue) is what the numbers rest on.
